Kiosk Hardware Comparison

Compass Project — evaluated against CSAH kiosk requirements (June 2026)

Analysis basis This comparison is grounded in the CSAH kiosk requirements document (June 2026), organized by the same category buckets: Physical Hardware, Display & Interaction, Software/Integration, Accessibility & UX, Operational/Nonprofit Fit, and Data Privacy & Governance. Items marked CONFIRM in the requirements were flagged as "TBD whether required" — they are surfaced here but not treated as hard requirements until confirmed.
Data Privacy is a non-negotiable, not a nice-to-have The requirements introduce a detailed data governance section informed by the LinkNYC case study — in which public kiosks were found to collect browsing history, retain camera footage without notice, and enable hyper-targeted advertising on homeless users. CSAH must contractually prevent these failure modes. Vendors whose software stacks transmit session or behavioral data to their own servers are a compliance risk regardless of hardware quality or price. IKE Smart City's advertising-funded revenue model makes them the clearest example of this pattern in this evaluation.
No Section 508 requirement CSAH is not federally funded, so federal accessibility law (Section 508) does not apply. WCAG 2.1 AA is the target standard. ADA physical compliance (48" max height, 30"×48" clear floor space) is required for all units.

Analytics and engagement data are central to IKE's value proposition to their city partners. Their platform is explicitly designed to report on usage. Limiting telemetry to hardware health only would require disabling their core platform — not a realistic contractual ask.

Verdict
Summary Best overall fit for CSAH across most categories — social service deployment precedent, built-in Zamok MDM, GSA pricing, and 3–4 unit nonprofit buyer comfort. The primary requirement this vendor does not satisfy out of the box is data privacy: Zamok's session data handling must be audited and contractually constrained before deployment. The ask is achievable — Zamok can likely be configured or contractually restricted to hardware-health-only telemetry — but it requires active negotiation, not assumptions. The closest real-world hardware analog (PHA lobby kiosks) and the most budget-friendly option. The data privacy concern here is structural, not incidental: integrated CMS/analytics platforms are built to retain session data on vendor servers, and that's an architectural conflict with the CSAH requirements. This doesn't rule MetroClick out, but it means the privacy negotiation is more complex than with Advanced Kiosks — you may need to disable or bypass core platform features to comply. The strongest data privacy posture of the three — by architecture, not contract negotiation. Hardware-only means no Olea software layer, no vendor session data collection, no camera activation pathway, and no law enforcement data access points. If data privacy is the top priority and budget is flexible (or the Boulder model comes in under $25K), this is the most defensible choice. The trade-offs are real: 4-unit orders are low priority for their sales team, and TCO requires assembling hardware + separate MDM quotes independently. IKE Smart City is a structural disqualification. Their advertising-funded revenue model means user behavioral data is the product — which is precisely the LinkNYC failure pattern this document was written to prevent. CSAH would not own the hardware, the software, the data, or the kiosk session experience. Beyond data governance, IKE's city partnership procurement model may not be available to CSAH as a nonprofit direct buyer. The social services equity references (Baltimore, Berkeley, St. Louis) are genuine, but those are cities deploying IKE at scale with the advertising model intact — not a template CSAH can follow. Do not pursue.

Connectivity: WiFi vs. Ethernet vs. Cellular

Preferred

Wired Ethernet

Highest stability, lowest latency, no wireless interference. Best choice for kiosks handling sensitive data and expected to run 24/7. Requires trenching conduit to run cable underground plus a certified network installer at each site.

Extra installation cost: $1,000–$2,500 per unit. Pursue wherever conduit to a nearby building is feasible.

Workable in lobbies

WiFi

Cost-effective where the host facility's existing network reaches the kiosk location. Can be affected by signal interference and congestion in crowded environments — which shelters and drop-in spaces often are. If WiFi is the only option, require WiFi 6 (802.11ax) support from the hardware vendor.

No extra infrastructure cost if the building's network reaches the placement location.

Outdoor fallback

Cellular (4G/5G)

The realistic option for any truly outdoor standalone placement with no nearby building infrastructure. All three viable vendors support wired Ethernet, WiFi, and 4G/5G cellular. Plan for cellular as primary in hard-to-reach placements, with WiFi as secondary if available.

Ongoing cost: $30–$80/month per unit ($360–$960/year). Budget as an operational line, not hardware.

CSAH site visit recommendation For each of your 3–4 locations, assess connectivity options during site visits before ordering hardware. If you can run conduit to a nearby building, wired Ethernet is the right call. If not, plan for cellular as primary with WiFi as secondary. Do not finalize hardware specs until site visits are complete — connectivity plan should inform the hardware order, not the other way around.
