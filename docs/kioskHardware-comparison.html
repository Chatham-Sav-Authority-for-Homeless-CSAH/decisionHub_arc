Assumptions
Analysis basis This comparison is grounded in the finalized CSAH kiosk requirements (July 2026), organized by category: Physical/Hardware & Display, Accessibility & UX Interaction, Software/Integration, Operational/Nonprofit Fit, Data Privacy & Governance, and Nice-to-Haves. Cells marked CALL CONFIRMED reflect data gathered from vendor conversations; cells citing a "formal quote" reflect written pricing documents. All other vendor cells represent market-estimate or unverified data — treat as starting points for quote conversations, not confirmed specs.
Data Privacy is a non-negotiable, not a nice-to-have The requirements introduce a detailed data governance section informed by the LinkNYC case study — in which public kiosks collected browsing history, retained camera footage, and enabled hyper-targeted advertising on homeless users. CSAH must contractually prevent these failure modes. Vendors whose software stacks transmit session or behavioral data to their own servers are a compliance risk regardless of hardware quality or price.
No Section 508 requirement CSAH is not federally funded, so federal accessibility law (Section 508) does not apply. WCAG 2.1 AA is the target standard. ADA physical compliance (48" max height, 30"×48" clear floor space) is required for all units.
New app requirement: arrow-key navigation (from MetroClick 2nd call, Jul 20 — now confirmed by both formal proposals) For outdoor freestanding kiosks, a 48"-max screen height and a large portrait display are mutually exclusive — at those sizes, portrait mounting puts the bottom of the screen too low for standing users. Both MetroClick (ADA Device) and Meridian (ADA NavPad with Audio Jack) solve this the same way: a physical directional keypad + Enter button + headphone jack mounted at compliant height beside the screen, decoupling ADA reach compliance from screen height. This means the CSAH kiosk web app must be fully navigable via keyboard arrow keys and Enter — not touch-only — for the keypad to work on either vendor's hardware. Confirm the same for Advanced Kiosks and Olea.
Formal quotes received — MetroClick & Meridian (Jul 22–23, 2026) Both vendors' phone-call estimates are now superseded by written quotes for specific models: MetroClick for 43" and 55" portrait kiosks (not the 32" originally discussed), Meridian for 32" and 43" Presenza kiosks. Two findings change the picture materially: (1) MetroClick's confirmed all-in price ($16,098–$17,118/unit) lands comfortably under budget with nonprofit discounts already baked in. (2) Meridian's confirmed all-in price ($32,626–$37,458/unit) is 2.2–2.7× the earlier $10K–$15K phone-call estimate and exceeds the $25K/unit ceiling at both sizes — driven by Meridian's own mZero software (Manage + Create SDK), SLA, and 3-year warranty being priced into the base quote rather than offered as a stripped hardware-only SKU. Advanced Kiosks' price list turned out to have the same problem. Olea still has no pricing at all — treat its figures as estimates only. Verdict
Summary Best value, confirmed in writing. The Jul 23 formal quote ($16,098–$17,118/unit all-in) lands comfortably under the $25K/unit ceiling and bakes in both nonprofit discounts (5% hardware, 10% SaaS fee) — no further negotiation required to hit these numbers. Confirmed models are 43" and 55" portrait (not the 32" originally discussed). ADA compliance is solved via a $500 physical keypad + audio jack accessory — an approach Meridian's own proposal now mirrors exactly, meaning the CSAH app must support full keyboard arrow-key + Enter navigation, not just touch.

Caveat: this quote is priced for MetroClick's own SaaS platform (Yearly SaaS Fee + Data Fee) — not the "blank Windows PC + ARC's own MDM" path previously recommended for data-privacy reasons. That alternative hasn't been re-quoted; the privacy posture assumed elsewhere in this doc needs re-confirming at MetroClick's actual pricing.

Outstanding: Google Maps domain-whitelisting test (pending from Michael), SLA tier pricing beyond the base SaaS fee, and warranty terms (2-year standard, not restated in the written quote — get 3-year written into the contract, not just discussed verbally).		 Best technical fit, worst confirmed price. Between the Jul 22 quote and the follow-up software requirements response, Meridian now has the strongest accessibility story of any vendor evaluated — integrated NVDA with automatic headphone activation, English and Spanish voices, accessible volume control — plus a confirmed 3-year warranty, a full ADA hardware kit (NavPad + audio jack, proximity sensor, cellular router), and Google Maps named explicitly as a supported multi-domain example. Camera policy is now resolved (disabled, no audience analytics). Meridian also proactively disclosed a new privacy finding — a default startup IP geolocation lookup — and proposed removing it, which is more transparency than any other vendor has offered.

None of that changes the confirmed all-in price: $32,626 (32") – $37,458 (43") per unit — 2.2–2.7× the earlier $10K–$15K phone-call estimate, and well over the $25K/unit ceiling at both sizes. The privacy-hardened profile that resolves most of the Data Privacy concerns is explicitly framed by Meridian as a custom software enhancement, not the platform's out-of-box default — confirm whether it's included in the quoted price or adds cost/timeline. The "hardware-only, no-mZero" configuration this doc previously assumed has still not been quoted.

Still outstanding: IP/NEMA coastal rating, social-service references (Goodwill appears among Meridian's general client logos, not a confirmed reference for this use case), and what "client to provide API keys through their own website" actually means for Web Speech API setup. The earlier procurement flag still applies: 16–20 week lead time. If budget can stretch, Meridian is now the strongest overall submission on paper — the question is whether it fits the grant, not whether it meets the requirements.
