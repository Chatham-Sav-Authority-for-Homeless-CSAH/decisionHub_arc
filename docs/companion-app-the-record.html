The Engagement Record

Every request, contact attempt, and outcome — timestamped, geolocated, and exportable.

Section 01

What the record is

An engagement is a documented encounter between outreach staff and one or more people. Whether the work began at a kiosk, on the phone, or in the field, it produces one engagement record.

The record carries where the encounter happened, when, which worker was there, who was present, what was provided, and how it ended.

One request produces at most one engagement. A single engagement can cover several people, each with their own record attached to it, and can carry more than one set of outcomes.

ClientTrack remains the system of record for the client.

This is the system of record for the engagement.

Section 03

What makes it defensible

Location is stamped by the system at the moment an engagement begins, which is the worker's location, wherever the encounter happens. Separately, each request carries where it came from: a kiosk tags its own location, and a phone call takes a location as text.

Time and identity are stamped at each step - when a request arrives, when it is claimed, when the engagement starts, when resources are recorded, when the record is closed, and when it is resolved.

The history is kept, not replaced. Every claim and release is retained with its reason. Every contact attempt is kept as its own timestamped record, so a record that took four tries shows four tries.

Consent is versioned. Each record stores that consent was given and the consent language the person was shown.

One note on timing: the app stamps location, time, and worker identity at the moment an engagement begins. A worker in the field may complete the remaining detail afterward. What is stamped at the moment is the encounter itself.

Section 04

What it supports, and what it doesn't

What it supports

  • That an engagement happened - where, when, and by whom
  • That the encounter was stamped when it happened
  • Who was present, and what was provided to them
  • Where people were referred, and to which provider
  • How the team performed, including how quickly requests were claimed, how long records took to close, what went unclaimed, and what arrived when no one was on duty after hours
  • Volume and pattern over time, by origin, by service, by provider, and by hour of day

What it does not support on its own

  • Citations or arrests. Showing that documented outreach changes enforcement outcomes requires enforcement data the app does not hold. Bringing the two together sits in the backlog.
  • Counts of distinct people, directly. The app counts engagements. Distinct-person counts come from the HMIS person IDs recorded during reconciliation, for records entered in ClientTrack - worked outside the app until integration.
  • Whether a placement held over time. Placement is verified once, during reconciliation.
  • A complete client history. That lives in ClientTrack.

Section 05

Who can see what

The kiosk keeps nothing. A request submitted at a kiosk is written to the shared database; the unit itself retains no copy.

Notifications carry no identifying detail. A worker is told the Request ID, the type of request, the location, and the time; nothing about the person.

Full detail is visible to the worker who claims the record, and to the Reconciliation role when the record reaches them. Teammates see only the type, location, elapsed time, and owner.

Changing a record is restricted. Reassigning or reopening work is limited to supervisors and administrators. Setting the HMIS status and confirming placement is limited to the Reconciliation role, with system administrators retaining access.

Reports carry counts, not identities. The impact rollup that goes to the city, the county, the state, and donors contains no names or identifying details.

Access follows role. Resource content, alerts, reconciliation, and reports are each restricted to the roles that need them.

Section 06

How long it is data kept

Records are kept for seven years, matching CSAH's retention period for paper records. A scheduled purge deletes them automatically once they pass that point, with no manual review by CSAH beforehand.
