The Flow

This is the path a help request takes, from the moment it arrives to the moment it is resolved. It can span more than one day and more than one person.

Section 01

The Home State

The duty toggle sits on Home. An outreach team worker turns it on to receive notifications; after nine hours on duty the worker is signed out automatically.

When an alert is active, a banner appears at the top of Home. A worker can dismiss it.

Similarly, the worker can see the following actions they can take depending on role and situation.

Requests (number of new unclaimed)

The Queue - Needs someone zone

Create a Help Request

The Intake form

My Work (Workers open items)

The Queue - Yours zone

Resource Directory

The directory

Start Engagement (Encounter)

New field engagement - skips the queue

Admin (role-gated)

The Admin area - Supervisor · Program Admin · System Admin · Reconciliation

Section 02

The Full Path

A request submitted at a kiosk travels the longest path, as it is the only one that includes every step. The other two ways work reaches a worker are shorter versions of it.

1

A help request is created

The person at the kiosk

A person submits at a kiosk. The intake form takes a name or alias and date of birth, a phone number, a CSAH Client ID if they have one, a description of themselves and of the help they need, party size, and whether an animal is with them. Date of birth and either a name or an alias are required. A person can decline to answer; if they decline everything, a physical description becomes required.

The kiosk tags its own location and keeps nothing - the request is created directly in the shared database and carries a Request ID.

The confirmation screen sets expectations on the request and shows 911, 211, and 988.

↓
2

The team is notified

The system

On-duty workers receive an in-app notification. The supervisor or on-call lead also receives a text, as a backup to the in-app notification.

The notification carries the Request ID, the type of request, the location, and the time. Nothing about the person leaves the system.

↓
3

Claims Request

Outreach Team Worker

The request appears unclaimed in the queue, sorted by age alongside everything else waiting. Any on-duty worker can take it.

The first claim wins. A worker who taps a moment later is told the request has already been claimed and by whom, and the row moves to their team view. Claiming requires a connection.

The worker who claims it sees the full intake record. Teammates see only the type, location, elapsed time, and owner.

↓
4

Starts engagement

Outreach Team Worker

Once the worker finds the person, they tap Start Engagement. The app records the time, & the worker.

The engagement type defaults to Initial Contact. The worker can change it to Follow-up, Wellness Check, or Coordinated Outreach.

↓
5

Multi-Person or Family Engagements

Outreach Team Worker

If more than one person is present, the worker adds each of them. Each person added gets their own record attached to the engagement, and party size is captured.

↓
6

Outcome

Outreach Team Worker

One outcome closes or holds the record. Only "Reached" leads to recording what was provided. The others either close the record or keep it open so the worker can try again.

Every attempt is kept as its own timestamped record, so the full history is available.

↓
7

What was provided

Outreach Team Worker

The worker records goods handed over and services provided. Each service carries its own status and the specific provider or location - one destination per service type. A referral records both the type of referral and the provider, and a provider is required whenever a referral is recorded.

The worker can open the resource directory from inside the engagement and send a resource's details or map directions to the person, by text or by a QR code the person scans.

Goods and services are both optional.

An engagement can close with neither recorded.

↓
8

Closed

Outreach Team Worker

When the worker is done in the app, the record is closed.

↓
9

Reconciliation

The Reconciliation role

The closed record moves to the reconciliation view, separate from the queue. There, the client is entered in ClientTrack, HMIS person/case ID is recorded, the HMIS status is set, placement is confirmed or declined and/or status is verified, the record moves to resolved.

This work is manual. The app does not connect to ClientTrack at launch.

Section 03

Additional Paths

A phone call

A call comes in and the worker creates the help request from the app, using the same intake form and entering the location as free text.

Then the worker chooses: Taking it self-assigns the request, which still appears in the queue tagged with their name - the path continues from step 4. Sending it to the team puts it in the queue unclaimed and notifies on-duty workers - the path continues from step 3.

The engagement that follows can be of any type, including an encampment visit when the encounter happens at one.

A phone contact is a contact attempt on the way to an engagement, not the engagement itself.

A field encounter

A worker canvassing meets someone.

The worker taps Start Engagement as they're in the field actively talking to someone. The app records their location and identity and assigns the work to them; the details can be entered afterward. This creates an engagement directly - there is no help request behind it. It is the shortest path and the most common one.

The only outcome is Reached, because the worker is there in person.

A worker who wants to first gather all the intake details can instead create a Help Request and then start Engagement.

Section 04

Moments That Branch Off

The person isn't there

The worker goes to meet someone and cannot find them. The engagement is still recorded - time, type, and the outcome "Not Found" - and the record closes.

Nobody claims it

If a request goes unclaimed for 30 minutes while workers are on duty, the supervisor or on-call lead is notified. If nobody was on duty when it arrived, the supervisor is texted immediately without waiting the 30 minutes. Escalation timing runs from the moment the record was created.

Requests submitted outside working hours are accepted and held in the queue, to be worked first thing the following morning.

The worker can't take it after all

A claim can be released. A reason is recorded, the request returns to the queue, and on-duty workers are notified again.

The person needs transport

From inside the engagement, the worker raises a transport task on that engagement. The task creates its own record, linked to both the request and the engagement, and goes out to on-duty workers using the same routing as requests. Only the Transport / support-service role can claim it.

If it goes unclaimed for 30 minutes, or if nobody in that role is on duty when it is raised, the supervisor is notified on the same terms as a request.

The call doesn't reach the person

No answer and Voicemail keep the record open so the worker can try again. Bad number, Contact not possible, and No returned phone call close it. Each attempt is kept with its own timestamp.

An alert is active

A banner appears at the top of Home carrying the alert's title, description, effective window, recommended actions, shelter and service locations with hours, transportation information, and emergency numbers. It is sent as a push notification only if that was enabled for the alert.

A worker can open the full list of active alerts from the banner.

Locations named in an alert link into the resource directory, or to one-time standup locations.

An alert does not create an engagement or a task.

The directory on its own

A worker can open the resource directory without an engagement. It is the same live dataset the kiosks use, navigated by category tiles and filtered by category, with eligibility shown as a badge. Resources are sorted by distance where the resource has a fixed location, and alphabetically otherwise.

From a resource the worker can send its details or map directions to the person by text or QR code, or display the number and dial it from their own phone.
