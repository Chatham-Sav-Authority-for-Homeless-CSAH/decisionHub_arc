Section 01

Executive Summary

Compass comprises two products built on one shared dataset. The kiosk network is the public-facing product; outdoor touchscreens where unsheltered people can find resources without a caseworker, a referral, or staff present. The Companion App is an outreach and support services team tool.

The Companion App is a mobile-first web app used by CSAH staff to receive, claim, and document engagements with unsheltered individuals, and to produce a defensible record that each engagement happened.

Work reaches an outreach worker three ways: a request submitted at a Compass kiosk, a phone call taken by staff, or a direct encounter in the field. All three converge on one engagement record carrying location, time, worker identity, and outcome.

Today that coordination runs on phone calls, texts, and memory. The app supports that coordination at launch and can replace it over time.

ClientTrack remains the system of record for the client. The app is the system of record for the engagement. At launch the two are connected manually during reconciliation rather than by integration.

Resource content and alerts are managed in one place and published to both the kiosk and the app.

Naming. "Companion App" is the descriptor in the executed Agreement and remains the contractual term. "Engage" is a proposed product name.

Section 02

Who uses it, and how they work

The Users

The Companion App is used by CSAH's street outreach team - at launch, roughly 11-15 people covering outreach, transportation, case management, housing, and administration.

They are one team wearing different hats rather than five kinds of users with five sets of needs.

An outreach worker who holds a client's active HMIS enrollment is that client's case manager; it is a hat, not a separate account. What differs between them is what each is permitted to do, which is set out in the roles and permissions section.

Transport is the one genuinely distinct role. A transport worker does not claim help requests and does not run engagements. They claim transport tasks raised by others.

How they work

Outreach work happens in the field. A worker is walking, driving between locations, or standing with someone. Most engagements begin as a field encounter, a worker canvassing meets someone, which is the highest-volume path.

Work is shift-based. A worker turns duty on to begin receiving notifications, and is signed out automatically after nine hours.

Connectivity is not guaranteed in the field. The app works offline and syncs when a connection returns, and documentation is often completed after the encounter rather than during it.

Not all of the work is mobile. Reconciliation, resource content, alerts, team oversight, and reports may be done at a desk. The app runs in a browser - on a phone in the field, on a computer in the office - and the same record is worked from either.

These conditions are why the app is built mobile-first, why it works offline, and why the accessibility baseline is written for one-handed outdoor use.

Section 03

Scope Boundary
  • The Companion App is used by CSAH staff. It is not a public or client-facing app.
  • ClientTrack remains the system of record for the client. The app is the system of record for the engagement. The two are connected manually during reconciliation, not by a live integration.
  • The app records what happens in the field. Ongoing case management stays in HMIS.
  • At launch the app serves CSAH's own outreach team as a single organization.
  • Access for partner agencies is a later expansion.
  • Transport is the only support service at launch.

Signing in

Every worker signs in to their own account, and the role they hold determines what they can see and do. A session ends automatically after nine hours on duty, and a worker can sign out at any time.

How accounts are issued and the sign-in experience itself are defined in design.

Section 05

Functional Capabilities

Each capability is a requirement, written as something the system does. Its number is what CSAH approves and what the build is tracked against, so the agreed scope and the delivered product stay the same thing. Capabilities are grouped by area and numbered. Each number is the unit of approval and is carried into our build backlog.

Help Requests & Intake

12 Capabilities
1.1
A person can submit a help request at a Compass kiosk. The kiosk retains no information — the request is created directly in the shared database.
1.2
Requests are submitted for one of four needs: Caseworker, Get an ID, Transport, or General Inquiry.
1.3
The intake form captures name, alias, phone, date of birth, CSAH Client ID, a self-description, additional detail about the help needed, party size, and whether an animal is present. The CSAH Client ID is optional and entered by hand from the person's ID card; when provided, it supports looking the person up in HMIS.
1.4
Date of birth and either a name or an alias are required. A "prefers not to answer" option is available, and if a person declines all identifying information, a physical description becomes required.
1.5
Location is recorded with every request — the kiosk tags its own location automatically; phone intake takes a location as free text.
1.6
Each request records that consent was given and which version of the consent language was shown.
1.7
The confirmation screen sets expectations on the request and displays 911, 211, and 988.
1.8
The system limits repeat submissions to prevent misuse. Blocked attempts are counted; no record is created and no personal detail is stored.
1.9
Requests submitted outside working hours are accepted and held in the queue, to be worked first thing the following morning.
1.10
A worker taking a phone call can create a help request from the app using the same intake form, entering the location as free text.
1.11
Every request carries a Request ID, which is how the record is identified throughout the system.
1.12
Every request records where it came from — kiosk, phone call, or field encounter.

Notification, Duty & Escalation

11 Capabilities
2.1
When a request is created, on-duty workers receive an in-app notification.
2.2
Notifications carry no personal or identifying information — only the Request ID, request type, location, time, and a prompt to open the app.
2.3
Each request also sends a text notification to the supervisor or on-call lead, as a backup to the in-app notification.
2.4
Notifications are routed by role and reach only workers who are on duty.
2.5
A worker turns duty on to begin receiving notifications. Duty is off by default and is separate from logging in.
2.6
A worker is automatically signed out after nine hours on duty.
2.7
A supervisor can see who is on duty but cannot change another worker's duty status.
2.8
The queue is visible to a worker whenever they are logged in.
2.9
If a request goes unclaimed for 30 minutes while workers are on duty, the supervisor or on-call lead is notified.
2.10
If no worker is on duty when a request is submitted, the supervisor is texted immediately without waiting the 30 minutes.
2.11
Escalation timing is measured from the moment the record was created.

The Queue & Claiming

12 Capabilities
3.1
Work appears in one queue with three zones: unclaimed work available to anyone, work this worker has claimed or started, and work claimed by teammates.
3.2
The queue is sorted by age. Each row shows the type of request, where it came from, the location, how long it has been waiting, and who owns it. Requests carry no priority ranking; urgency is conveyed in the description of the need.
3.3
Any on-duty worker can claim an unclaimed request.
3.4
The Transport / support-service role can claim tasks only, not requests.
3.5
Claiming is instant — no form to complete and no HMIS step.
3.6
The first claim wins. A worker who taps a moment later is told the request has already been claimed and by whom, and the row moves to their team view.
3.7
A worker can release a claim. A reason is recorded, the request returns to the queue, and on-duty workers are notified again.
3.8
The worker who claims a request sees the full intake record. Teammates see only the type, location, elapsed time, and owner.
3.9
Every claim records the time and the worker's identity, and the full history of claims and releases is retained.
3.10
A worker creating a request from a phone call can take it themselves or send it to the team. A self-claimed request still appears in the queue, tagged with that worker's name.
3.11
Claiming a request from the queue requires a connection.
3.12
The home screen is role-aware: it shows the count of unclaimed requests and the worker's own open items, and for the Transport / support-service role it shows transport tasks in place of requests.

Engagements

12 Capabilities
4.1
An engagement is a face-to-face encounter with one or more people, and is the record of what happened.
4.2
Starting an engagement records the time and the worker's identity.
4.3
Every engagement records the worker's location at the moment it starts.
4.4
Intake information from the request is carried into the engagement and shown to the worker.
4.5
Every engagement is typed. The type defaults to Initial contact and the worker can change it to Follow-up, Wellness Check, Coordinated Outreach, or Encampment visit.
4.6
The types available depend on where the work came from — an encampment visit cannot originate from a kiosk request.
4.7
Additional people can be added to an engagement. Each person added gets their own record attached to that engagement, and party size is captured.
4.8
One request produces at most one engagement; a single engagement can cover several people.
4.9
A worker in the field can start an engagement without a help request. The app records their location and identity and assigns the work to them; details are entered afterward.
4.10
A worker in the field can instead create a help request using the full intake form.
4.11
When a worker goes to meet someone from a kiosk or phone request and the person is not there, the engagement is still recorded — time, type, and the outcome "Not found."
4.12
A phone contact is recorded as a contact attempt on the way to an engagement, not as the engagement itself.

Engagement Documentation

12 Capabilities
5.1
Every engagement is closed with a single recorded outcome.
5.2
The outcome is chosen from a fixed list, each with a defined meaning.
5.3
An outcome of "Reached" leads to recording what was provided. All other outcomes either close the record or hold it open.
5.4
Outcomes representing an unsuccessful contact attempt keep the engagement open so the worker can try again. Every attempt is kept with its own timestamp, so the full history is available.
5.5
Goods provided are recorded from a checklist.
5.6
Services provided are recorded from a checklist, each with its own status.
5.7
For each service, the worker selects the specific provider or location. One destination is recorded per service type.
5.8
Goods and services are both optional — an engagement can close with neither recorded.
5.9
A referral records both the type of referral and the specific provider. A provider is required whenever a referral is recorded.
5.10
Everything recorded is tied to the person it was provided to, with the time and the worker who recorded it.
5.11
A referral to a resource such as shelter or housing carries forward to reconciliation, where the placement is either confirmed as verified or recorded as declined.
5.12
A field encounter closes as "Reached" — the worker is present with the person.

Tasks & Team Support

10 Capabilities
6.1
From inside an engagement, a worker can raise a transport task to the team on that existing engagement. Transport is the only support service at launch.
6.2
A task creates its own record, linked to both the request and the engagement it came from.
6.3
Tasks are sent to on-duty workers using the same notification routing as requests.
6.4
Only the Transport / support-service role can claim a task.
6.5
Tasks appear in the unclaimed zone of the queue alongside requests.
6.6
For the Transport / support-service role, the app surfaces its tasks in place of requests.
6.7
A task can be released. A reason is recorded, the task returns to the queue, and on-duty workers are notified again.
6.8
If a task goes unclaimed for 30 minutes while the Transport / support-service role is on duty, the supervisor or on-call lead is notified.
6.9
If no one in the Transport / support-service role is on duty when a task is raised, the supervisor is texted immediately without waiting the 30 minutes.
6.10
Escalation timing for tasks is measured from when the task was created.

Resource Directory

11 Capabilities
7.1
Workers browse the same live resource directory the kiosks use.
7.2
The directory is navigated by category tiles.
7.3
Resources can be filtered by category. Eligibility is shown as a badge and is not used as a filter, matching the kiosk.
7.4
A resource shows its address, hours, phone number, eligibility, and service notes.
7.5
Resources are sorted by distance where the resource has a fixed location, and alphabetically otherwise.
7.6
From a resource, a worker can send that resource's details to the person's phone - by text, or by a QR code the person scans.
7.7
From a resource, a worker can send map directions from the worker's current location to the person's phone - by text, or by a QR code the person scans.
7.8
Texts are sent to a phone number the worker enters at the time.
7.9
A worker can display a resource's phone number and dial it from their own phone.
7.10
The directory is available on its own and from inside an engagement, where it is used to record a referral.
7.11
Resource entries display a photo of the building.

Alerts

7 Capabilities
8.1
An active alert appears as a persistent banner at the top of the home screen.
8.2
An alert is sent as a push notification only if it is enabled for that alert.
8.3
A worker can open the full list of active alerts from the banner.
8.4
An alert carries a title, description, effective window, recommended actions, shelter and service locations with hours, transportation information, and emergency numbers.
8.5
Locations named in an alert link into the resource directory, or to one-time standup locations.
8.6
A worker can dismiss an alert, which clears the banner for that session.
8.7
An alert does not create an engagement or a task.

Reconciliation

9 Capabilities
9.1
A record moves through three states: in process, closed once the worker is done in the app, and resolved once HMIS reconciliation is done.
9.2
Closed records awaiting reconciliation are collected in a dedicated reconciliation view, separate from the queue.
9.3
Each record carries one HMIS status - entered, not applicable, or pending - and a status must be selected before the record can be resolved.
9.4
The HMIS person ID is entered manually via free text.
9.5
Placement is confirmed as verified or recorded as declined during reconciliation.
9.6
Resolving a record captures the HMIS status, the HMIS person and case IDs, the consent version, the time of closing, and the worker who closed it.
9.7
The CSAH Client ID captured at intake supports looking the person up in HMIS.
9.8
Reconciliation is performed by a dedicated Reconciliation role. System administrators retain access.
9.9
Reconciliation is manual - the app does not connect to ClientTrack at launch.

10.3

10.10

10.11
Access to each area is controlled by role.

Reporting & Exports

Reports present counts. They do not contain names or identifying details about the people served.

Two reports can be produced.

The operations report is for supervisors and administrators running the team day to day.

The impact rollup is for reporting at an executive level — to the city, the county, the state, and donors.

Reporting & Exports

9 Capabilities
11.1
Reports present counts of requests and engagements.
11.2
Data is stored at daily granularity and rolled up daily, monthly, and yearly.
11.3
An operations report shows per-worker quantities.
11.4
Team totals add requests left unclaimed, requests submitted after hours, and requests that arrived with no one on duty.
11.5
An impact rollup shows engagements logged, goods by category, service referrals by provider, kiosk-to-engagement conversion, and a map of encounters shown as pins.
11.6
Reports export to CSV and Excel.
11.7
Exports can be filtered by date range, location, service category, and worker.
11.8
Export field names align to HUD vocabulary.
11.9
Reports are available to system administrators, program administrators, and supervisors.

Per-worker measures in the operations report

Engagements logged by origin (kiosk, phone, field) · requests claimed · goods by type · services by type · referrals by provider · handoffs to triage · counts by hour of day · claimed response time · claim to start of engagement · claimed to closed · closed to resolved

Platform Behavior

4 Capabilities
12.1
The app works offline and syncs when a connection returns. Claiming a request that another worker may also be claiming requires a connection.
12.2
A worker can view their account and profile details, including the role they hold.
12.3
A worker can log out manually.
12.4
Records are retained for seven years and then deleted automatically on a scheduled purge, with no manual review.
